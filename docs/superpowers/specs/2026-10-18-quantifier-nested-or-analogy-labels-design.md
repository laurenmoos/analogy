# Quantifier, Nested Disjunction, and Analogy Labels Design

## Intent

Remove benchmark interference from tactic-generation hygiene, make nested disjunction recovery
deterministic, and make analogy predictions measurable. The ordinary search budget remains identical
between arms, and analogy remains a stuck-only recovery mechanism.

## Required behavior

### Quantifier tactics

- The shared pre-Lean filter accepts `intro x` and `intros` when the focused target begins with `∀`
  or `forall`, in addition to implication and negation goals.
- `intros` remains rejected on goals that cannot introduce binders.
- The tactic card mentions quantifier introduction when the current target is universal.

### Early recovery and missing candidates

The two missing Lean submissions in `nested_or_01` are caused by the existing early-recovery rule:
after `left` and `right` both create SAT-refuted successors, the loop stops before the remaining
candidate pool is evaluated. Those candidates were not filtered. The search record and report will
explicitly list the remaining candidates as skipped by early all-disjunct recovery, so they no longer
disappear silently. This preserves the requested early-recovery behavior.

### Nested disjunction forced move

When the rescue hypothesis has exactly three flattened disjuncts, such as
`h : A ∨ (B ∨ C)`, the deterministic forced move is a three-way split:
`rcases h with hA | hB | hC`. Names are derived from proposition atoms and freshened against the
current context. Binary disjunction behavior remains unchanged. Longer disjunctions may continue to
use the existing binary fallback; general N-way splitting is not required in this change.

### Context-aware Lean card

The compact card uses visible hypothesis names from the focused goal rather than a literal `h`.
It selects a relevant disjunction hypothesis and conjunction hypothesis when available. A
three-disjunct hypothesis produces a three-way `rcases` example. The card stays generic: it contains
syntax examples, not a solution-specific `exact` hint, and remains bounded to avoid material token
growth.

### Benchmark

Remove `iff_intro_05` and every synthetic problem from the hard benchmark and report expectations.
The fixed benchmark contains only entries loaded from the checked-in lean4game-logic corpus:
`not_intro_05`, `or_intro_05`, and `or_intro_06`. Quantifier and three-way-disjunction behavior are
verified with focused unit/integration fixtures, not presented as benchmark evidence. The matched
runner always executes both arms fresh and never combines either arm with an earlier attempt.

### Analogy prediction labels

The analogy prompt requires `predicted_state` to be either literal `SOLVED` or the exact immediate
focused Lean goal expected after the tactic. Valid analogy generations receive exactly one label:

1. `predicted_no_change`: normalized `predicted_state` equals the current focused goal. The tactic is
   not sent to Lean; the generation consumes one analogy-generation slot and the model is asked for a
   different prediction/tactic.
2. `prediction_matched`: Lean completes when the prediction is `SOLVED`, or Lean returns an OPEN
   state whose normalized focused goal equals `predicted_state`.
3. `prediction_mismatched`: all other Lean results after a predicted change, including Lean errors,
   discarded successors, `SOLVED` predictions that remain open, and OPEN predictions that complete.

Invalid JSON/schema generations have no prediction label because they do not contain a valid analogy
output. Reports show the label for every valid analogy output.

### Deterministic duplicate feedback

The harness owns an ordered state-local tactic ledger. Duplicate retries include a short bounded list
of canonical tactics already tried at that state, in first-attempt order. The list is generated by
code, never by the model, is capped at eight tactics and 240 characters, and therefore has stable,
small token cost. Ordinary, forced, and analogy submissions share the same ledger.

### Reporting and rerun

Run one matched comparison: the same three corpus problems, the same ordinary-search configuration,
once without analogy and once with stuck-only analogy. There is no repeated-run selection, search,
or reporting. Reports and persisted experiment files do not expose random-generation controls.

The report separates problems with an observed countermodel/SAT prune from problems with no observed
countermodel. Classification is derived from recorded search events across both arms, not from
problem names. Each section reports per-problem outcomes and baseline-stuck metrics. The complete
combined report retains prompts, generations, prediction labels, retries, filter and early-skip
records, Lean outcomes, countermodels, and forced moves.

## Non-goals

- No ablation arm.
- No `tauto`/`itauto` baseline.
- No semantic interpretation of quantified goals by the propositional checker.
- No private chain-of-thought reporting.

## Verification

Use test-first changes for each behavior, run the full suite and compile checks, perform a fresh
read-only review, then run the matched corpus-backed comparison and regenerate the combined report.
